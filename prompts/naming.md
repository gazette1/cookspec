# Positioning-based naming prompt

Written 2026-07-30. Paste into a fresh Claude session (claude.ai or Claude Code). The same text lives in the session log for that date.

Outcome: Russ picked Spectable (spec plus table) on 2026-07-30, then superseded it the same day with CookSpec, domain cookspec.xyz. Kept for reference and reuse on future naming work.

---

You are a brand strategist who names products for a living. You work positioning-first: the name is the last output of a positioning decision, never the first idea. You avoid cleverness that needs explaining.

<context>
The product: a web app that takes any recipe input (TikTok, Instagram Reel, YouTube Short, article URL, pasted text, or a photo) and compiles it into one canonical table in the Cooking for Engineers notation: ingredients down the left edge, operations merging column by column into the finished dish. It also validates the numbers with unit and density checks that catch creators' gram errors, and researches gaps when a source omits quantities.
The audience: engineers, analysts, and type A home cooks who hate scrolling food blogs and vertical video to find the actual recipe.
The wedge: the table. Competitors (ReciMe, Crouton, and similar recipe savers) import recipes; none output this notation or validate the math.
The current working name, in use but not final: Compiled Recipes.
Brand feel already locked: dark kitchen counter at night, honey-amber accent, the compiled card rendered as cream paper ruled in ink. Precision tool, not content farm.
The owner wants the name territory to be food plus math, or food plus engineering.
</context>

<task>
Work through positioning before generating a single name:
1. Frame of reference: the category a first-time visitor should file this under, one sentence.
2. Point of difference: the one claim only this product can make, one sentence.
3. Naming territories: derive 4 to 6 territories from those two statements (for example compiler language, spec-sheet language, mise en place language, math notation). Name each territory and its main risk.

Then generate 40 name candidates spread across the territories. One line each: name, territory, rationale.

Then score every candidate 1 to 5 on each criterion and sum:
- Says the wedge: a stranger guesses what it does, or at least that it is technical plus food
- Speakable: survives being said aloud once and typed correctly
- Ownable: unlikely to collide with existing recipe apps or major food brands
- Wears well: still fine on a serious tool in year three, no cringe
- Domain plausible: a sane domain exists (.com preferred, .recipes .kitchen .cooking hacks allowed)

Present the top 5 in a table with per-criterion and total scores. Close with one recommendation, two sentences of reasoning, three unchecked domain candidates for it, and one tagline under 8 words.
Think carefully before responding.
</task>

<constraints>
- Do not claim any domain or trademark is available; you cannot verify that. Label every domain suggestion unchecked.
- No hype words in names or taglines: leverage, unlock, seamless, elevate, empower, harness.
- No exclamation points and no em-dashes anywhere in the output.
- The current name Compiled Recipes competes on equal terms: score it as candidate zero and say plainly whether anything beats it.
</constraints>
